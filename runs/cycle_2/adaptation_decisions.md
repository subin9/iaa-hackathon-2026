# Adaptation decisions — what carries over from Part 1

One row per component. "Carry over" is one of: AS-IS (used unchanged), ADAPTED (method kept, parameters or inputs
changed), RE-ESTIMATED (method kept, values re-fitted on emerging data), BORROWED (taken from the developed market
because emerging data cannot support it), DROPPED. Evidence names the file or table that supports the decision.
Remaining uncertainty says what could still be wrong and, where known, how much the predicted level moves if it is.
Exact validation scores are in `baseline/validation.md`; this table records the decisions.

## What changed in this market (summary)

The emerging book is written through brokers (70% vs 25%), insures younger people (17-24 is 20% of training and 25%
of test exposure vs 13% in the developed market), and after January 2025 is three-quarters new business from one
transferred portfolio, so the model extrapolates an 8%-of-exposure training segment to 77% of the test book. The
climate is warmer and wetter with twice the hail days, and the peril mix follows: more storm and glass/hail, less
escape of water. Motor claims are 30% more frequent and 40% cheaper than in the developed market (third-party and
bodily-injury costs are roughly half), buildings cost is dominated by fire (45% of cost from 7% of claims) and swings
40% between the two years, contents is 20% cheaper. Expense and tax loadings are higher (fixed 85 vs 45 CU, broker
commission 20% vs 15%, cat reinsurance 12% vs 6%) and the comparison-site commission is missing. The data system
differs too: age in bands, gender recorded, no credit band, mileage or telematics, a 360-day exposure convention
alongside the day-count one, free-text noise in occupation, and a 2023 year that only contains policies which
survived into 2024.

## Open decisions for the team

1. **Trend candidate A (flat) or B (developed trend, two years)** for the final submission; B adds 4 / 13 / 11% by line.
   Evidence that would settle it: the 2025 book's first quarters, or a market inflation index for repair and
   bodily-injury costs.
2. **Gender as a rating factor** (F-GENDER): predictive in motor (17-24 men claim 1.7× women); regulatory question.
3. **The 36 pre-inception claims** (F-CLM-PREINCEPT): mis-keyed loss dates or genuine prior-insurer claims of the
   acquired book? Immaterial to levels, material to the audit trail.
4. **Survivor sample in 2023** (F-SURVIVOR-2023): confirm how the extract was built; if lapsed 2023 policies exist,
   adding them changes the 2023 level and the primary validation fold.
5. **Comparison-site commission** (F-EXPENSE-MISSING): needed for the premium build of 5% of the book.
6. **ncb_years exceeding years_licensed** on a fifth of motor rows (F-NCB-GT-LICENCE): definition of ncb_years in
   this market.
7. **New-business relativity for property lines**: the data (77 / 102 new-business claims) cannot say whether the
   acquired book should be priced at, above or below renewals; the pre-model motor and contents excess (+17% /
   +25% frequency) is the only signal. A view from the broker's own loss history would settle it.
8. **Whether to borrow the developed market's new-business and age relativities** as a prior for the property lines
   (shape only), given how thin the emerging segments are. Not done in the baseline.
