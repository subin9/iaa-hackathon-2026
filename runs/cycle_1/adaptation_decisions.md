## What changed in this market (summary)

The emerging book is the same three products sold mostly through brokers (70% vs 25%) to a younger customer base (17-34: 39% of training rows, 50% of 2025) in a flat, low-lying six-region geography, with an older vehicle fleet (no cars under three years) and more renters (43% vs 30%). Customers behave differently on cost rather than on frequency: motor frequency is 30% higher but every motor peril costs 40-50% less, so the motor burning cost is a quarter below the developed market; contents frequency is 15% lower at the same severity; buildings has the same frequency with a fire load (45% of cost on 70 claims) that the developed market does not show. Reporting is faster in every line. Cost signals in the expenses table point the same way as the claims: higher fixed expense (85 vs 45 CU), higher broker commission (20%), lower premium tax (8%), and a doubled catastrophe reinsurance load (12% of property risk premium) consistent with the flood and fire exposure. The 2025 test book is 3.9× the 2024 exposure and 76% new business transferred from a broker; on everything declared (prior claims, no-claims years, licence years, channel, region) it resembles the insurer's own new business, which in training runs 18% above renewals in motor after age standardisation.

## Open decisions for the team

1. Trend candidate: the +3/+6/+5% borrowed trend or zero trend (two files, R-0505). The developed market's own 2024 drop (motor −9% completed) argues for caution.
2. IBNER: whether to apply any uplift to the motor level for 2024 open-claim reserving (0 to +6%); local validation cannot settle it.
3. New-business relativity for property: fit freely (77 / 102 claims) or shrink to the renewal level; motor's +18% age-standardised excess is credible enough to keep.
4. Use of gender as a rating factor in this market (available only here; regulatory question).
5. The 36 pre-inception claims: excluded; confirm with the claims system whether loss dates should be corrected.
6. ncb_years vs years_licensed inconsistency on 16-22% of motor rows: which field is authoritative for the acquired book.
7. Comparison-site commission is undefined in expenses although 5% of the book uses that channel.
8. Whether the buildings fire load of 2023-2024 is the expected level or two unusual years (reinsurance view would help).
