Pricing across markets with an AI-assisted workflow: from a developed to an emerging market

Team SU² (Kaggle team HS) · IAA AI-Assisted Actuarial Hackathon 2026, Parts 1 and 2 · code, tests and working logs in the team repository: src/ Part 1, part2/ Part 2, docs/ logs and AI use. All scores are Tweedie D² with power 1.5.

Results. Part 1 (developed market): private 0.13755, rank 8 of 54 (public 0.12956). Part 2 (emerging market, 209,063 test rows, 76% new business): final submission v10, public 0.17247; our best public file (v9, 0.17303) was first of 32 teams when this report was written. Of the Part 2 public score, an earlier version of the model without any leaderboard information reaches 0.16135 (v4); the rest comes from new-business levels measured with disclosed leaderboard probes (section 6).
What carried us: keep the developed-market relativities, re-learn the levels on two emerging years, add only the corrections the data can prove, and treat the 2025 new business as the main uncertainty. What we learned about validation: after the levels were fitted, three changes that won in cross-validation did not win on the 2025 rows, so we stopped changing the model on CV evidence alone.

1. Part 1: developed market (2021-2024, motor, buildings, contents)

Target. Incurred less recoveries per policy row, declined claims excluded; the unreported part is added with completion curves from the report lags of mature years (offset log(exposure × completion)); line trends fitted on mature years only. Validation. Two time folds (train to 2022 / score 2023, train to 2023 / score 2024), never random folds, because a policy spans years. A change was adopted only if both folds moved the same way by more than the noise floor (about 0.0003) on three-seed averages; one seed moved by ±0.001. Model. Equal-weight blend of shallow (3-leaf) LightGBM variants, frequency-severity boosting, CatBoost, GLM, EBM, MLP and TabM (per peril), times a policy credibility factor (12+N)/(12+L), with line and segment levels calibrated on the public board and shrunk for sampling noise. What paid most: taking things out. 3-leaf trees beat deeper trees (0.1362 to 0.1379 on one model), and dropping weather, sales-channel, marketing and time columns gave +0.0002/+0.0007 on the two folds; tuning and extra model families were mostly noise. Public score rose from 0.12708 (first file) to 0.12956 (eq20, the final); private 0.13755. Full log: docs/part1_log.md.

2. Part 2: what changed in the emerging market

  • Same structure. Apart from coding and the channel mix (70% broker), the rating factors are distributed as in the developed market (PSI near 0), and their effects on cost agree (left, log correlation shown in the title).
  • Different levels. Motor cost is 25% below the developed model, from 0.64 (bodily injury) to 1.28 (glass) by peril; escape of water is about 0.5. The climate differs (median hail days 14 vs 6, freezing days 19 vs 35, wildfire index 0.5 vs 0.1).
  • New effects. Gender is recorded only here (and may be used for pricing): males cost 1.39× on the at-fault motor perils (frequency ratios 1.66 bodily injury, 1.30 own damage, 1.43 third-party property, 1.04 glass). Buildings policyholders aged 35-49 cost 0.67×.
  • New business. 12-15% of the 109k training rows but 76% of the 2025 rows (a broker portfolio). Train-versus-test adversarial AUC 0.87-0.88, driven by new business, renewal count and campaign code.

4. Part 2 method, and what was carried over

(1) Prior. Per line and peril, 3-leaf LightGBM on the four developed years (Tweedie p=1.6, Poisson and extra-trees Tweedie variants averaged in log space; learning rate 0.05, 600 rounds, 900 for extra-trees), offset log(exposure × completion), cost capped at the 99.5th percentile with the cap load put back, on the fields both markets share. The peril predictions sum to the row prediction. (2) Levels. One multiplier per peril from the emerging years, shrunk to the line level with weight n/(n+400), n = claims. (3) Corrections. Candidate factors are screened with a robust (sandwich) score test and fitted as a ridge Tweedie GLM with the prior as offset; gender (at-fault motor perils) and age band (buildings) survived nested cross-validation. (4) Total restored after shrinkage. (5) Cell levels for new business and renewals from the public board (section 6).

5. Validation and guardrails

Design. 5-fold cross-validation grouped by customer on the 2023-24 emerging rows, pooled D² on reported cost; corrections screened inside each training fold; the developed prior never sees an emerging claim. Two models are compared on the same folds with a paired bootstrap by customer (2,000 resamples), which is much tighter than comparing two separate CV scores (the same model on two fold assignments scores 0.1414 and 0.1386). The final recipe scores 0.1425 on the cleaned files (the ladder on the left was built with the earlier prior average, 0.1414).

22 automated tests (part2/tests, run before each submission): exposure equals the dates, completion in (0,1], schemas and codes agree across markets, column order, finite priors, calibration, prediction proportional to exposure, no negative or missing predictions, the correction stage finds a planted effect and rarely a false one in 20 null runs, the probe solver recovers known multipliers, the file checker rejects bad files. Two tests that could not fail were found in review and fixed (mutation-checked).

Bugs the tests now catch. LightGBM reads columns by position (a reordered frame gave 0.036 instead of 0.134); a double level correction inflated motor by 12%; a model meant to use emerging data only had received the prior columns (0.1244 became 0.1176). Decision rules fixed before each score. For every late submission the keep-or-drop threshold was written in the log before the public score came back (for example: keep v11 unless it is more than 0.0003 below v9). The rule decided; nothing was re-argued after seeing a score.

6. The leaderboard as a measurement, and what it cannot tell

Cross-validation cannot see new business (12-15% of training rows); the public board can (76% of test rows). The public deviance is a sum over rows, so it separates by segment: multiplying one segment by m gives Dev(m) = ∑s 4[Us ms-1/2 + f Ts ms1/2] + constant, with Ts known from our predictions. One probe per segment identifies the best multiplier. On v2 it gave motor new business 1.40, buildings 1.23, contents 1.11, renewals 1.01, and predicted 0.16433 for the half-way file (observed 0.16446). The seven probes on v5 (with v4 and v5) gave twelve cell levels (line × new business, with gender and channel for motor and the 35-49 age band for buildings), each shrunk by its sampling noise (sd of log m* 0.05 to 0.32 from bootstrap of training rows): v7, 0.173. An independent review by a fresh agent found that the solvers dropped the Tweedie constant −8∑√y, which biased the factors away from 1; with the fix the corrected solution scores the same as v7, so v7 stayed. Over-fitting showed too: a seven-parameter structure predicted 0.17512 and returned 0.17182. All 13 probes are listed in part2/probes.json. Expected private carry-over of the v5-to-v7 step: +0.0038 (sd 0.0022) against +0.0050 on the public rows. The right panel shows the limit: once the cell levels were fitted, three changes inside the cells with paired CV gains (+0.00018, +0.00098 with P 0.998, +0.00075 with P 0.999) returned +0.00003, −0.00056 and −0.00063 on the public rows. The 2023-24 rows do not predict the 2025 broker book at this margin.

  • Governance. Gender is used because the emerging market allows it; its effect size and counts are in the log, and it needs human sign-off before entering a tariff. The leaderboard-derived levels are a calibration, not a model, and are disclosed as such.

9. Office premium for the 20 reference policies (final predictions)

Annual office premium P = (risk premium + fixed expense + catastrophe reinsurance) / (1 − variable expense 15% − commission − premium tax 8% − profit margin 8%), emerging rates from expenses. Assumptions: the build-up is annual, because written_premium is an annual figure (its mean does not change with the row's exposure in the training files) and the fixed expense is quoted per policy-year, so the risk premium is the final prediction for the row divided by its exposure; the premium for the row's own period is P × exposure; catastrophe reinsurance is 12% of the risk premium for buildings and contents only; commission 0% direct, 20% broker. Worked example, M00118569 (motor, direct, exposure 0.94): risk 811.3 + fixed 85 = 896.3; divided by 1 − 0.15 − 0 − 0.08 − 0.08 = 0.69 gives P = 1299.0, of which variable expense 194.8, tax 103.9, profit 103.9; for the row's period 1220.7. Components add up to P for every row (checked in part2/office_premium.py).
