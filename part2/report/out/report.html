Pricing across markets with an AI-assisted workflow: from a developed to an emerging market

Team HS · IAA AI-Assisted Actuarial Hackathon 2026, Parts 1 and 2 · code, tests and working log: repository iaa-hackathon (src/ for Part 1, part2/ for Part 2)

Summary. Part 1 (developed market): Tweedie D² 0.13755 on the private leaderboard, rank 8 of 51 (best 0.13923). Part 2 (emerging market, 209,063 test rows, 76% new business): public D² to be filled. What carried Part 2: keep the developed-market relativities, re-learn the levels, add only the corrections two emerging years can prove, and treat new business as the main uncertainty. In cross-validation on the emerging rows a model trained on emerging data alone reaches 0.118, the Part 1 model ported unchanged 0.134, the final pipeline 0.141 (a constant rate per line: 0.078). New business is not visible in that cross-validation (12-15% of the rows) and moved the public score most; section 6 shows how we measured it and the limits of that measurement.

1. Part 1 in brief (developed market, 2021-2024, three lines)

Target: incurred less recoveries per policy row, declined claims excluded; ultimate cost via completion curves from report lags of mature years; offset log(exposure x completion); trend per line fitted on mature years (+3.4%, +6.3%, +5.4% a year). Equal-weight blend of shallow (3-leaf) gradient boosting, frequency-severity boosting, CatBoost, GLM, EBM and neural nets, with credibility adjustment per policy and a level calibration on the public leaderboard (public 0.12956, private 0.13755). What we learned and reused in Part 2: the data are close to a multiplicative structure (3-leaf trees beat 15-leaf trees by 0.0016), weather, sales and marketing columns add noise, averaging more models stops paying at about 0.0002, and levels are the cheapest thing to get wrong.

3. What changed in the emerging market

  • Same structure. Apart from coding, the channel mix (70% broker) and new business, the rating factors are distributed as in the developed market (PSI near 0), and their effects on cost agree (figure, left).
  • Different levels. Motor cost is 25% below the developed model, from 0.64 (bodily injury) to 1.28 (glass) by peril; escape of water is about 0.5 in buildings and contents. The climate differs (median hail days 14 vs 6, freezing days 19 vs 35, wildfire index 0.5 vs 0.1), and 2025 is colder and less hail-prone than 2023-24 (freezing days 29 vs 21 and 17; hail days 11 vs 14 and 17).
  • New effects. Males cost 1.39x females on the at-fault motor perils (frequency ratios 1.66 bodily injury, 1.30 own damage, 1.43 third-party property, 1.04 glass); buildings policyholders aged 35-49 cost 0.67x. Neither effect exists in the developed market (gender is not recorded there).
  • New business. 12-15% of training rows but 76% of 2025 rows (a broker portfolio; 83% carry a campaign code, 19% of training rows do). The train-versus-test adversarial AUC is 0.87-0.88 and is driven by exactly these columns.

4. Method, and what changed from Part 1 to Part 2

(1) Prior. Per line and peril, shallow LightGBM (3 leaves; Tweedie p=1.4, Poisson and extra-trees variants averaged in log space) on the four developed years, offset log(exposure x completion), cost capped at the 99.5th percentile with the cap load put back, on features both markets share (vehicle details, weather, marketing, history and premium left out; new_business kept after it added +0.0002 and +0.0006 on the two Part 1 validation years). The peril predictions sum to the row prediction. (2) Levels. One multiplier per peril from the emerging years, shrunk to the line level with weight n/(n+400), n = claims. (3) Corrections. Candidate factors are screened with a robust (sandwich) score test and fitted as a ridge Tweedie GLM with the prior as offset (the penalty equals 100 claims of prior weight); gender (at-fault motor perils) and age band (buildings) survived nested cross-validation. (4) Total. The training total is restored after shrinkage. (5) New-business level, from the public leaderboard (section 6).

Also tried and rejected: boosting corrections on the prior (0.1339 vs 0.1337), the developed prediction as a feature, GLM-initialised trees, weather in the prior (helps glass and storm per peril, not the line D²), vehicle details and make codes (no effect, and their codes are not comparable across markets).

5. Validation and guardrails

Design. 5-fold cross-validation grouped by customer on the 2023-24 emerging rows (a customer is never on both sides), pooled Tweedie D² (p=1.5) on reported cost, as in the competition; corrections are screened inside each training fold. The developed prior never sees an emerging claim.

22 automated tests (part2/tests): exposure equals the dates; completion in (0,1], 1 for test rows; developed and emerging schemas and codes agree; feature column order; finite priors; calibration on training rows; prediction proportional to exposure; no negative or NaN predictions; the correction stage finds a planted effect and, in 20 null runs, rarely a false one; the probe solver recovers known multipliers; the submission checker rejects bad files.

Bugs the tests now catch. LightGBM reads columns by position, so a reordered frame gave D² 0.036 instead of 0.134. A double level correction inflated motor predictions by 14% (found by comparing mean predicted rates with the previous version). A model that was supposed to use emerging data only had silently received the prior columns (fixed: 0.1244 became 0.1176).

6. Using the leaderboard as a measurement, and what it showed

Public D² is a sum over rows, so it is separable by segment: Dev(m) = ∑s 4[Us ms-1/2 + f Ts ms1/2], where Ts is known from our predictions and Us is not. One probe per segment (multiply that segment by 1.2) identifies Us and gives the best multiplier m*s = Us/(f Ts); the public share f cancels. On version v2 the method gave 1.40 (motor new business), 1.23 (buildings), 1.11 (contents) and 1.01 (renewals), and it predicted 0.16433 for the half-way point; the leaderboard returned 0.16446. This is a disclosed use of the leaderboard (ten submissions a day; every probe is listed in part2/probes.json); the sampling error of a multiplier is about 3% and we shrink them (see final version).

7. Uncertainty and limitations

  • Small perils. Fire (70 claims), subsidence (52), flood (80 and 86) are shrunk to the line level; their 2025 level is uncertain by more than 30%. The 99.5% cap load rests on 9,258 claims.
  • New business is the largest uncertainty: 76% of 2025 rows against about 1,000 training claims. The developed market gives +8% (motor) and +13% (buildings), the emerging rows more (motor A/E 1.23, standard error 0.15), the leaderboard more again. Whether the uplift sits in the broker channel (A/E 1.25 on 555 claims against 0.94 on 206 for direct) is unresolved.
  • Year effects. 2024 claims are 80-97% complete and open case estimates were low in the developed market; the renewal level was confirmed on the leaderboard (multiplier 1.01). The 2025 weather shift is not modelled.
  • Governance. Gender is used because the emerging market allows it. It is a rating correction whose effect size and sample counts are in the log, and a human must review it before it enters a tariff. All submissions and the final choice are the team's decision.
